# Vacancies → VA & Home-Health Housing: A Starter Primer

*East West Homes · first draft, 2026-10-31 · written to learn from and to seed the podcast agents. Everything marked **VERIFY** must be checked with the agency or a primary source before money moves.*

## The one idea to hold onto

**Medicaid and the VA almost never pay rent directly.** They pay for *care*. Rent comes from one of three places:

1. **A housing voucher** (HUD-VASH, Housing Choice Voucher, Mainstream/811). The housing authority pays most of the rent.
2. **The resident's own income**, usually SSI, SSDI, or a VA pension or disability check.
3. **A provider that master-leases the unit** (a group-home operator, a transitional-housing nonprofit, a hospital's recuperative-care program) and builds the rent into its per-diem or grant budget.

So the business isn't "get paid by the VA." It's **becoming the landlord that care organizations and voucher programs depend on**: units that pass inspection, are accessible, get turned fast, and come with a manager who picks up the phone. The website already says that's what you do ("someone discharged from care with nowhere to go … they are the ones brought to us"). This turns it into a formal product line.

## Three ways to play it (from least to most risk)

| Model | Who pays you | What you need | Risk / margin |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Program landlord.** List vacancies with VA and voucher programs | Housing authority (voucher) plus tenant share; SSVF for deposits and incentives | Units that pass HQS/NSPIRE inspection, a 1-year lease, a fast-responding PM | Low risk, market-level rent, steady occupancy |
| **B. Master lease to a provider.** Lease a whole house or building to a licensed operator | The provider, often on a 3–5 year triple-net or modified-gross lease | Accessible layouts, sprinklers and egress to code, zoning that allows the use | Medium risk, often above-market rent, one tenant to collect from |
| **C. Become the operator.** You hold the license and deliver the service | Medicaid waiver per diem, VA per diem, private pay | State licensure, staff, compliance, insurance | High risk and high margin. This is a different business. |

**Recommendation:** start with A on every current vacancy and pursue B with one or two providers per market. Don't do C until A and B are working and you've hired someone who has run one.

## VA lanes

### 1. HUD-VASH (vouchers plus VA case management): *the easiest win*
- A veteran gets a Housing Choice Voucher from the local housing authority, plus a VA case manager who stays with them. You sign a normal lease. The housing authority pays its share every month, and the case manager is your phone call if something goes wrong.
- **Action:** call the **HUD-VASH program at each VA Medical Center** (VA Pittsburgh Healthcare System; Birmingham VA Health Care System; for Sumter, the Columbia SC VA, Wm. Jennings Bryan Dorn) and the matching housing authority (Pittsburgh: HACP / Allegheny County HA; Birmingham: HABD / Jefferson County; Sumter Housing Authority). Ask: *"Do you keep a landlord list, and what is your payment standard for a 1BR/2BR in my ZIP?"*
- Vouchers frequently go unused because veterans can't find units. A landlord who brings ready units is welcome.

### 2. SSVF (Supportive Services for Veteran Families)
- Nonprofit grantees use VA money to rehouse veterans fast: deposits, first month's rent, short-term rent help, and **landlord incentive payments**.
- VA guidance (2022–2024) ties landlord incentives to a **1-year lease; month-to-month doesn't qualify**. One VA deck shows a sample first payment of about $5,000 (2-month incentive plus first month's rent). **VERIFY** current amounts with your local grantee.
- **Action:** find the SSVF grantees serving Allegheny County, Jefferson County AL and Sumter County SC (va.gov/homeless/ssvf lists them) and get on their landlord lists.

### 3. Grant & Per Diem (GPD): transitional housing
- VA pays nonprofits a per diem per veteran per night for transitional beds (typically up to 2 years). The nonprofit needs buildings.
- **Your angle:** master-lease a multi-unit property (Burgess Court–type assets, or a small building in Pittsburgh) to a GPD grantee. Their grant budgets include rent.
- Secondary sources report about $318M in GPD for FY2027 (announced Sept 2026). **VERIFY** at va.gov.

### 4. VA Medical Foster Homes (MFH)
- A private caregiver lives in a home and cares for up to 3 veterans who would otherwise need a nursing home. VA provides Home-Based Primary Care in the home.
- **Today the veteran pays the caregiver directly** (rates negotiated, often in the low thousands per month). The Cleland-Dole Act authorized VA to pay room and board for some veterans for 5 years, but GAO reported VA had **not started paying as of mid-2025** because its systems weren't ready. **VERIFY** current status. If payments start, demand jumps.
- VA says MFH is operating or in development at 150+ medical centers and is expanding in FY2026.
- **Your angle:** you're the landlord to the caregiver. An accessible single-family home with a first-floor bedroom and bath for the caregiver, and room for 2–3 veterans. Ask each VAMC's MFH coordinator whether caregivers are struggling to find suitable homes.

### 5. VA Community Residential Care (CRC)
- VA-approved private homes (often state-licensed personal care homes) where veterans live and pay from their own income. Overlaps with Model C. Watch it, don't lead with it.

## Home-health / Medicaid lanes

### 6. Intellectual & developmental disability (IDD) group homes: *the biggest master-lease market*
- **Pennsylvania:** ODP (Office of Developmental Programs) waivers fund "Community Homes" (licensed under 55 Pa. Code Ch. 6400, generally up to 4 people) and Life Sharing. Providers constantly need **single-family homes with 3–4 bedrooms, wide doors, a no-step entry, and often sprinklers**. Many lease rather than own.
- **Alabama:** ADMH (Dept. of Mental Health) ID waiver. **South Carolina:** DDSN (Dept. of Disabilities and Special Needs) residential (CTH-I / CTH-II).
- **Action:** ask 3 ODP providers in Allegheny County what they pay to lease a 4BR ranch and what modifications they need. That one call tells you whether your vacancies fit.

### 7. Older adults & physical disabilities: Medicaid HCBS
- **PA Community HealthChoices (CHC)** (managed by UPMC, AmeriHealth Caritas/Keystone First, PA Health & Wellness) funds in-home care, not rent. Members use SSI or a voucher for rent. The managed-care plans' **housing specialists** look for accessible units.
- **Nursing-home transition / Money Follows the Person:** people leaving nursing homes need accessible, affordable units *now*, and transition coordinators hunt for them. Same story with **AL's Elderly & Disabled waiver** and **SC's Community Choices waiver**.
- Rent math: SSI federal benefit is about **$994/mo in 2026** (**VERIFY**), so without a voucher, rent must fit roughly 30–50% of that. Pair these tenants with Mainstream / 811 / HCV vouchers.

### 8. Hospital discharge & medical respite (recuperative care)
- Hospitals lose money keeping unhoused patients who are medically ready to leave. Medical respite programs (hospital- or nonprofit-run) pay per diem for short-term beds with nursing check-ins.
- Several states now use **Medicaid 1115 "health-related social needs" waivers** to pay for short-term post-hospital housing and reentry housing. **VERIFY** what Pennsylvania's current 1115 approval covers and whether Alabama or South Carolina have anything similar.
- **Action:** ask UPMC, AHN and UAB social work / care-transitions departments whether they contract for recuperative-care beds.

### 9. Reentry housing
- This fits the founders' story directly. PA's Medicaid reentry work and county reentry programs (Allegheny County Jail Collaborative) look for landlords. Often voucher- or grant-backed.

### 10. Mid-term furnished rentals for travel nurses (no program needed)
- 30–90 day furnished leases near UPMC/AHN (Pittsburgh), UAB (Birmingham) and Prisma Tuomey (Sumter). Typically rents above unfurnished market. Good for units that don't suit Models A or B.

## Guardrails (read before calling anyone)

- **Fair Housing.** You can't screen tenants by disability, veteran status (in some localities), or source of income where it's protected (Pittsburgh and Allegheny County protect source of income). Partnering with programs is fine. Refusing non-program applicants or steering people is not. Get a one-time review from housing counsel.
- **Zoning & group homes.** The Fair Housing Act requires reasonable accommodation for group homes of people with disabilities, but local definitions of "family" and occupancy caps still matter. Check zoning before signing any master lease.
- **Inspections.** HUD moved voucher inspections to **NSPIRE-V**. Your full-renovation standard is an advantage here.
- **Licensing is the provider's job under Model B.** Keep it that way. Your lease should require the tenant to hold and keep all licenses, and give you an exit if they lose one.
- **Accessibility retrofits** (ramps, grab bars, roll-in showers) may be fundable: VA SAH/HISA grants for the veteran, Medicaid waiver home-mod funds, or local programs.
- **Insurance.** Tell your carrier the use. Group-home and transitional uses can change the policy.

## 30-day learning plan

| Week | Do | Output |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | List every current vacancy: bedrooms, accessibility (steps, bath on main floor, door widths), market | Vacancy fit sheet |
| 1 | Call HUD-VASH and SSVF in all 3 markets | Payment standards, landlord-list sign-ups |
| 2 | Call 3 ODP/IDD providers (PA) and 1–2 each in AL and SC | Lease rate and spec sheet each provider needs |
| 2 | Call 1 hospital care-transitions team per market | Is there paid demand for discharge beds? |
| 3 | Call VAMC Medical Foster Home coordinators | Caregiver housing demand; status of VA payments |
| 3 | 1 hour with housing / fair-housing counsel | Lease addendum, guardrails |
| 4 | Pick 2–3 units and run Model A or B on them | First placements; numbers for an investor update |

## Questions the podcast agents should hunt for

1. Who is master-leasing single-family homes to IDD/ODP or behavioral-health providers, at what rent, and on what lease terms?
2. What are landlords actually earning from HUD-VASH and SSVF (incentives, payment standards, vacancy loss, damage claims)?
3. Has VA started paying Medical Foster Home room and board? Any operator economics?
4. Which hospitals or health plans pay for post-discharge or recuperative-care housing, and how is it contracted?
5. Medicaid 1115 housing-supports waivers in PA, AL and SC: what is funded, and who are the housing partners?
6. Mistakes: provider defaults, licensing losses, zoning fights, neighbor lawsuits, insurance problems.
7. Funding: who lends on or invests in healthcare-adjacent housing (CDFIs, impact funds, LIHTC with supportive set-asides)?
