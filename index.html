Who we're for

At the end of the day, this is about people. It's about the single mother who needs a safe home for her kids. It's about families and first-time buyers who've been overlooked by everyone else. Most of the industry chases the same comfortable buyer — we're here for the people society too often forgets.

That's why we get creative with owner financing and rent-to-own paths, like the families becoming homeowners through our work in Birmingham. And it's not just who we build for — it's who we build with. We intentionally hire minority- and women-owned contractors and subcontractors, the skilled people who never got their shot.

We believe everyone deserves an opportunity — a home, a livelihood, a stake in their community — and that belief drives everything we do.

One of our founders lived in Section 8 housing. She knows exactly what separates a landlord who meets the minimum from one who does the work, and she would not build this company any other way. Today 21 of our 57 doors are subsidized — more than a third of the portfolio, by choice.

Everyone deserves a place to call home — and a fair shot at building something of their own.

We're a Pittsburgh-based real estate investment and development company, but our roots run deeper than any single market. We started by taking on the properties others walked away from — distressed, forgotten houses — and revitalizing them into safe, quality, affordable homes.

Over time our work has grown to span the full spectrum, from careful restorations to premium homes and ground-up new construction. Through all of it, one thing has never changed: we do the work right, and we do it for the people who need it most.

What we do

Every unit we own gets a new roof, updated HVAC and plumbing, tile, new fixtures and a renovated kitchen. Every single one — at market rent or subsidized, no difference.

We do it that way because of who lives there.

Why

One of our founders lived in Section 8 housing. She knows what it is to be handed the version of a home that somebody decided was good enough for you — the landlord who patches instead of fixes, the kitchen nobody has touched in thirty years, the repair that never comes. She would not build a company that does that to anyone.

So we don’t. A family holding a voucher walks into the same house a market tenant would, finished to the same standard, maintained by the same crews. It is not a favor and it is not charity. It is the baseline, and it should have been the baseline all along.

It is also, as it turns out, how you build something durable. Units that are done properly pass inspection, hold tenants for years, and do not hand the next owner a bill. Agencies place their hardest cases with us because we are reliable. UPMC signs leases with us for the same reason. Our capital partners keep investing because it performs.

None of that is why we started. It is why it works.

Subsidized & affordable housing

More than a third of our doors, in every market we operate: Housing Choice Vouchers in Pittsburgh, Allegheny County, Jefferson County and Sumter; Rapid Re-Housing and Permanent Supportive Housing placements for people coming out of homelessness; and leases with UPMC for patients who need a stable address as much as they need treatment.

Premium renovations

The same crews, the same standard, applied to higher-end homes. These projects carry the margin that lets us hold affordable units for the long term without ever cutting what goes into them.

Ground-up construction

In Pittsburgh we build new, including through the Land Bank — taking vacant, tax-delinquent lots and putting occupied homes back on them. Six under construction, six more in design.

Who builds it

We hire minority- and women-owned contractors and tradespeople who get passed over elsewhere. For many, our projects have meant steady income when other doors stayed closed. The people who build these homes come from the same neighborhoods as the people who live in them.

One standard, every door. Everything else follows from that.

Communities & partnerships

Great housing doesn't happen in isolation. We work hand in hand with the organizations and programs that make quality, affordable homes possible — and we get creative to open doors that would otherwise stay shut.

Who we work with

21 of our 57 doors are subsidized — more than a third of everything we own, in every state we operate. Not a side program, and not a Pittsburgh experiment. These are the agencies and institutions we work with to do it.

These are working relationships, not logos on a page. Each one puts a real family in a real home.

Housing Authority of the City of Pittsburgh

We are an approved Housing Choice Voucher landlord. HACP inspects our units and pays housing assistance directly each month.

Allegheny County Department of Human Services

We house residents through the county’s Rapid Re-Housing and Permanent Supportive Housing programs — people moving out of homelessness into a stable address. We also take placements through the county’s landlord program.

UPMC

We work directly with UPMC to house people in their care — coordinating with their case managers and accepting UPMC-paid rent, so housing and treatment hold together.

Sumter Housing Authority

Voucher-supported households across our 16-unit Burgess Court community in South Carolina.

Jefferson County, Alabama

Housing Choice Voucher households across Birmingham and Bessemer — the market where the company started, and subsidized from the first house.

Pittsburgh Land Bank — Urban Redevelopment Authority

A repeat developer with the Land Bank, cleared through its vetting on every acquisition and working across Spring Hill, Garfield, and the East End. The Land Bank exists to return vacant and tax-delinquent property to productive use. That’s not a box we check — it’s the work itself.

Renting shouldn't be the end of the road

Through owner financing and rent-to-own paths, we help families who've been shut out of traditional lending become homeowners — building equity, stability, and something to pass on. It's already happening: families in our Birmingham market are on the path to owning the homes they live in.

When neighborhoods thrive, everyone benefits. That's the future we're building — one home, one family, one community at a time.
